Why it is shaped like this

Each of these is a decision, not a detail. They are the things worth arguing with before any of it is built.

  1. Service is chosen first, not third. Today the patient picks a date, an hour and a language while the wizard header reads “pricing shown once you pick a concern”. The price and the session length only arrive a step later — and the session length is what decides whether their chosen slot clashes with anything. Choosing the service first means the header is honest from the first screen.
  2. One dialog, two views, never two stacked dialogs. View details swaps the dialog’s contents and offers ← Back to all services. Two reasons: the app’s public dialog is not portalled and applies a backdrop blur, so a second dialog opened inside it would be positioned against the first panel’s box rather than the screen; and two stacked sheets leave a reader unsure which Escape closes what.
  3. The card is the one the rest of the app already uses. Same component as /conditions, /home-visit and the patient dashboard’s own booking screen — it gains a “choose” mode rather than being copied. Two cards is how two screens grow two ideas of what a service looks like.
  4. A photograph is positioned, not cropped. The card frame is 4:3 and the detail frame is 16:9, so one baked crop cannot serve both. “Post-Surgery Rehab” here is positioned at 75% 30% — open its details and the subject stays in frame in both shapes.
  5. No photograph is a designed state. “Paediatric Physiotherapy” has no cover: it draws the same tinted panel at the same height, so the card reads as a photo not yet chosen rather than a photo that failed to load.
  6. Nothing multi-session appears here. A course of treatment comes from a recommendation a therapist wrote after a session they ran, so the picker offers single sessions and single visits only. Showing programmes would be a price list a patient cannot buy from.
  7. With one bookable visit there is no picker at all. The home-visit flow shows it as already chosen, with its photograph and price and a details link. Today that dropdown is hidden entirely and the patient never sees what they bought. Switch the toggle above to Three for the other shape.
  8. The visit is chosen above the pincode, not below it. The “yes, we visit you” message has to say whether travel is charged on top, and that depends on which visit was chosen — so the choice has to come first. Pick each visit in turn and watch that sentence change.
  9. Continue appears once the service is chosen, exactly as this screen already waits for a date, an hour and a language before offering it. No dead button and no browser tooltip — the app has never let a browser default speak to a patient.
  10. A deep link still lands chosen. Arriving from /conditions or the dashboard on /book?category=… opens Step 1 with that service already in the slot — and still never guesses one when the link names none.
