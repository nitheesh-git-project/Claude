MoveRestore Physiotherapy is a physiotherapy practice in India offering video
consultations and home visits through its own web platform, with patients in
India and, by video, abroad (including the EU/UK). Patients pay online
(Razorpay) or in cash at home visits. Independent physiotherapists deliver the
sessions for a revenue share. Partner hospitals refer patients for a
commission. The platform holds health data (condition questionnaires, a pain
map, session notes, uploaded medical reports).

The business is **not yet registered**; the entity name, registered address,
registration number and jurisdiction city are blank in every document.

## Documents in this pack

The internal **breach response plan** and **list of data processors** are
also available on request.

## What the platform already does (for context)

- **Consent:** cookie consent (analytics and marketing opt-in, equal Accept and
  Reject, recorded); agreement to Terms and Privacy at signup, recorded with
  version and time; existing users asked again when the Terms change;
  telehealth consent per booking; a parent or guardian confirmation for any
  booking for someone under 18.
- **Rights:** each user can download a PDF of their data from their profile,
  and ask for account deletion (handled within 30 days; clinical, payment and
  audit records are kept, marked as a closed account).
- **Grievances:** a complaint form with acknowledgement within 48 hours and
  resolution within 30 days, tracked against those deadlines.

## Questions we would like answered

1. **Laws covered.** Are the pages sufficient for: the DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP
   Rules 2025; the IT Act 2000 and SPDI Rules 2011; the Consumer Protection
   (E-Commerce) Rules 2020; the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (if they
   apply to physiotherapists); and the GDPR / UK GDPR for patients abroad?
2. **Health data retention.** How long must clinical, payment and audit records
   be kept after an account is deleted? We currently keep them indefinitely,
   marked as a closed account, and would like a defined period.
3. **Refunds.** Is "full refund 24 hours or more before the session, none
   after" acceptable under consumer law? The window is a setting the clinic
   can change.
4. **Children.** Bookings for under-18s require the booker to confirm they are
   the parent or legal guardian. Is this "verifiable consent" under DPDP s.9,
   or is more needed?
5. **Data processors.** Supabase (database, Mumbai), Razorpay, Google
   (Calendar, Meet, Analytics), Resend (email, USA), Meta (WhatsApp). Do we
   need anything beyond each supplier's standard DPA?
6. **Therapists.** Independent-contractor classification, the
   non-solicitation clause, and the data-processing clause (see the notes at
   the end of that draft).
7. **Hospitals.** Whether a per-session referral commission raises
   fee-splitting / anti-kickback concerns, and the data-sharing roles (see the
   notes at the end of that draft).
8. **Limitation of liability** in the Terms of use and both agreements.
