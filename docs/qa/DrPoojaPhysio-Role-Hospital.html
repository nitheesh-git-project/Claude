1. Who this is, and what must exist first

The account. A referring partner - a hospital, a clinic or a practice - that sends patients to this practice under a commercial agreement and takes a share of what those patients pay. They are provisioned by an admin; they do not self-register.

The one word that matters. A referral is a hospital sending a patient under a commercial agreement. An invite is one patient telling another. They are different things with different tables, different rewards and different screens, and the words stay apart. The link a hospital sends its referred patient is a registration link, never an "invite link". A screen in this flow saying "invite" is a P2 - one back office cannot have two things called an invite.

1.2 How to record a result

Mark every step Pass, Fail, Blocked or N/A. Raise a failure with: the step id, what you did, what you expected, what happened, a screenshot, any console error in full, how many times out of how many it reproduced, and which account you were signed in as.

Severity. P0 - money, clinical data or access control is wrong: stop and report. P1 - a main journey is broken with no workaround. P2 - wrong with a workaround. P3 - cosmetic.

1.3 Calling a route without a terminal

Several steps call an API directly, because this application enforces its rules twice - once in the screen, once in the route - and a hidden button proves only the first. Sign in as the person the step names, press F12, open Console, paste and press Enter:

const r = await fetch("/api/some/route", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
  body: JSON.stringify({ some: "value" }),
});
({ status: r.status, body: await r.text() });

For an anonymous call use a private window - running it where you are signed in sends your cookie and proves nothing. For a different partner use a second browser profile, not a second tab; tabs share cookies. Never paste the URL in the address bar: that sends a GET, these are POSTs, and you will get 405 and think you found something.

2. Becoming a partner

HO-01 - The enquiry on the public page · P1

Before an account exists, a hospital arrives as a lead.

Do this

  1. Signed out, in a private window, open /hospitals.
  2. Read the page, then fill in the enquiry form:
FieldValue
Hospital / clinic nameQA Partner Hospital A
Contact personQA Hospital Admin A
Emailqa.hospital.a@example.test
Phone+91 98765 43230
CityBengaluru
Message200-bed orthopaedic unit, ~30 post-discharge physio referrals a month.
  1. Submit.
  2. Submit it again, immediately. And again, and again - a dozen times.

Expect

  • Step 3 confirms, with a sentence saying somebody will be in touch. No account is created and nothing signs you in.
  • Step 4: after several attempts you are held off, with a message that carries no numbers (the cap and the window are configuration) and no blame - a limit is reached by a shared office address or a retrying connection far more often than by anybody doing anything wrong. "Too many attempts" reads as an accusation: P3.
  • The wait itself is specific, because it is measured: the screen says roughly how long.
  • This is a real route, not a browser-side database insert. Confirm in the Network tab that submitting hits /api/hospitals/inquiry. A public write with no server-side door has nothing to put a limit in: P1.

HO-02 - The admin creates the account · P1

As the Master Admin, open People → Partners and create Hospital A from the details in §1, with a 15% revenue share. Then create Hospital B at 10%.

Expect

  • A password is generated and shown once. Write it down now.
  • A referral code is generated. Write that down too - Step HO-05 needs it.
  • The generated password stays on screen. It must not vanish mid-sentence because the screen refreshed for its own write. If it disappears before you have copied it, that is a P1: the clinic has just issued a credential nobody has.
  • The password does not appear in the activity log. The log is readable by every admin - who reset what and when is the part with audit value.

HO-03 - First sign-in · P2

Do this. Sign in at the ordinary sign-in screen as Hospital A with the generated password. Then set your own password through the account screen, and sign in again with the new one.

Expect

  • You land on /hospital/dashboard.
  • Once you have set your own, the clinic's stored copy of the issued password is cleared. An admin looking at your row should see you are no longer on a password they issued.
  • No screen anywhere shows you a password you chose yourself. That value does not exist in the database - only a hash does. A screen offering to display it is a P0, because it would mean something is storing it.

3. The hospital's dashboard

HO-04 - Read the Overview · P1

Do this. As Hospital A, open /hospital/dashboard and read it top to bottom before tapping anything.

Expect

  • A strip of four figures, then the feed, then quick actions - the same shape as every other dashboard in this product.
  • The figures cover referrals made, patients converted, sessions delivered and what is owed to you.
  • The sidebar word for money is Earnings. Money owed to somebody is Earnings, in the therapist's sidebar and in yours. "Revenue & Payouts" or any third word for the same thing is a P2 - it reads as a different concept.
  • Each figure opens the rows it counted, and the count there matches.
  • Back to Home is in the sidebar in all three renders: expanded, collapsed rail, and the mobile drawer.

4. Referring a patient

HO-05 - Refer a patient for a video consultation · P1

Do this

  1. Open Refer a patient.
  2. Fill in Referral 1 from the table in §1.
  3. Submit.

Expect

  • The phone number is required and is validated the way every other number in this app is. A referral is the one flow where the clinic must reach somebody who has no account yet - they agree a time by phone and only then send the registration link. A referral form with no phone field is a P1.
  • The preferred language is captured.
  • The referral appears in My Referrals as awaiting the clinic.
  • No account has been created for the patient, and nothing has been emailed to them by this app.

HO-06 - Refer a patient for a home visit · P1

Do this. Refer Referral 2, choosing the home visit delivery mode, with the address from §1.

Expect

  • The address is collected, and its pincode is checked against the service areas before the referral is accepted.
  • Try an unserviceable pincode - 560025: the form says the clinic does not visit that address and does not accept the referral.
  • Now drop the connection and try again on a serviceable one.

Expect a third answer: we could not check. Not "we do not visit your address". A failed lookup reported as an unserviceable address is a P1 - it tells a partner the clinic refuses a patient it would happily see.

HO-07 - Withdraw one · P2

Do this. Withdraw Referral 2, then look at the list.

Expect. It is withdrawn, visibly, with its reason if one was asked for. Then try to withdraw it a second time from the console:

const r = await fetch("/api/hospital/withdraw-referral", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
  body: JSON.stringify({ referralId: "PASTE_THE_ID" }),
});
({ status: r.status, body: await r.text() });

Expect a refusal or a no-op, never a second withdrawal. Then re-refer Referral 2 so the rest of the plan has it.

HO-08 - One partner cannot see another's referrals · P0

As Hospital B, in a second browser profile:

TryExpect
Open My ReferralsEmpty. Hospital A's two referrals are not there
Open one of Hospital A's referrals by URLRefused, and no patient detail renders
Call /api/hospital/withdraw-referral with Hospital A's referral idRefused
Open Hospital A's revenue screen by URLRefused

Any of Hospital A's patients' names, phone numbers or conditions reaching Hospital B is a P0 that stops the run. These are referred patients' identities and medical issues.

5. What the clinic does with a referral

These steps are the admin's, and they are here because the hospital has to be able to see the result.

HO-09 - The admin reads the referral · P1

As the Master Admin, open People → Partners → Patient Referrals.

Expect

  • Both referrals are listed under Hospital A, with the patient's phone number and preferred language directly under their name. That is what the screen is for: the clinic rings before it links.
  • The capacity note beside the list renders. If the phone is missing but the note is there, the column is simply not on this database yet - that is tolerated by design, and the rest of the card must still render. The whole card failing because of one column is a P1.

HO-10 - Assigning a slot obeys the platform's own rule · P1

Do this. As the admin, assign Referral 1 a session time.

Expect

  • The control is the same compact calendar and hour chips a patient sees, obeying the same 12-hour lead time.
  • It is not a raw date-and-time box. An admin could otherwise promise a referred patient a slot the platform's own rule refuses - two answers to "when can this be booked", in the one flow where the person choosing is not the person who lives with it. A native `datetime-local` here is a P1.
  • A time like 6:52 cannot be produced. Slots start on the hour.
  • It is not a dialog: the slot is chosen against the referral it sits inside.
  • Try to assign a slot inside the lead time. Refused, by the route and not only by the grid - confirm by calling /api/admin/assign-referral directly with an early time.

HO-11 - The registration link · P1

Do this. Have the admin send the registration link for Referral 1. Copy it, and open it in a private window.

Expect

  • The screen says registration link, not "invite link", anywhere it names itself.
  • It carries the patient's details forward - they are not typing their own name and condition again.
  • The patient sets a password and lands in their own dashboard with the agreed session already there.
  • Open the same link a second time after it has been used: it does not create a second account.
  • Open a link with a mangled token: refused, with a sentence saying the link is not valid.
  • Now drop the connection and open a good link. It must say we could not check, not this link has expired. A good link reported as expired is a P1 - it sends a patient to ring the hospital about a link that works.

6. Money

HO-12 - The share is earned on net revenue · P0

Do this. Have Referral 1's patient pay for and complete a session. Then open the hospital's Earnings.

Expect

  • The commission is 15% of net revenue on that session, not of the list price and not of the gross before refunds.
  • Travel is not in it. On Referral 2's home visit, the ₹150 travel fee is a pass-through reimbursement paid to the therapist in full. A commission taken on travel is a P0.
  • A session that is paid but not yet delivered contributes nothing.
  • Every figure carries an (i) explaining what it means, and each says whether it moves with the date range or is true right now. A balance that shrinks when you narrow the date range is a P1 - a debt does not stop existing outside a filter.

HO-13 - A refund reverses the commission · P0

Do this. Have the admin refund Referral 1's session. Then look at Earnings again.

Expect

  • The commission is reversed. A refunded session was cancelled, so there is nothing to take a share of.
  • Check the same figure on the admin's Money screens: the two must agree. The clinic's own share and the partner's share disagreeing across two screens is a P0 - there is one place this is calculated precisely so they cannot.

HO-14 - What the hospital cannot see about the money · P1

7. The hospital's profile

HO-15 - Edit what you own · P2

Do this. Open the profile screen, change the contact person, the phone and the city, and save. Reload.

Expect. They save and survive the reload. Then confirm what is not editable here: the revenue share, the referral code, and the account's own approval state. A partner editing any of those is a P0.

8. What a hospital must never be able to do

HO-16 - The wrong dashboards · P0

Each signed in as Hospital A:

TryExpect
Open /admin/dashboardRedirected to `/get-started` - never to /admin/login, which would confirm the back office exists and name its door
Open /patient/dashboardBounced. One account carries one role
Open /therapist/dashboardBounced
Open /bookThe wrong-account panel, routing a hospital to refer rather than to book - not a booking form and not a bare error
Open /book-home-visitThe same panel

A hospital that can complete a booking is a P0: that session belongs to no patient dashboard and money has moved.

HO-17 - The route sweep · P0

In the console, as Hospital A:

const routes = [
  "/api/admin/approve-account",
  "/api/admin/assign-referral",
  "/api/admin/update-hospital-revenue-share",
  "/api/admin/create-booking",
  "/api/admin/settle-therapist-payout",
  "/api/admin/refund-session-partial",
  "/api/admin/clear-activity-log",
  "/api/therapist/reveal-contact",
  "/api/therapist/suggest-session",
  "/api/patient/condition-profile/export",
  "/api/appointments/create",
  "/api/appointments/complete-session",
];
console.table(await Promise.all(routes.map(async (route) => {
  const r = await fetch(route, {
    method: "POST",
    headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    body: "{}",
  });
  return { route, status: r.status, body: (await r.text()).slice(0, 90) };
})));

Expect 403 on every one. Not 404, not 500, and not a 400 that reads as "give me better arguments" - that is a route that checked the body before it checked who was asking. Any 200 is a P0 that stops the run.

HO-18 - The public referral-code door · P0

A referral code identifies a partner, and anybody can type one. Two things must be true.

Do this, in a private window with no session at all:

const codes = ["QAHOSPA", "QAHOSPB", "AAAA01", "AAAA02", "AAAA03", "AAAA04",
               "AAAA05", "AAAA06", "AAAA07", "AAAA08", "AAAA09", "AAAA10"];
for (const code of codes) {
  const r = await fetch("/api/hospital/verify-code", {
    method: "POST",
    headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    body: JSON.stringify({ code }),
  });
  console.log(code, r.status, (await r.text()).slice(0, 120));
}

Expect

  • A valid code answers with the partner's name and nothing else - never a list of their referrals, never a patient's name, never a medical issue.
  • After a handful of attempts the door holds you off, so codes cannot be enumerated one guess at a time.
  • The refusal is a 429 with a wait, and whatever calls it must treat that as "we could not ask" rather than "that code is invalid". Confirm on the registration screen: a rate-limited check must not tell a real referred patient their link is bad.

HO-19 - Suspension ends the session · P0

Do this

  1. As the admin, suspend Hospital A.
  2. In the hospital's still-open browser, reload the dashboard.
  3. In the same browser, try to refer a patient from the console.
  4. Un-suspend.

Expect

  • Step 2: bounced to the suspended screen.
  • Step 3: refused. A suspended account's own routes refuse it too - the session was ended, not merely redirected away from. A suspended partner still referring patients or reading referral detail is a P0.
  • Step 4 restores them exactly as they were, with their referrals, their code and their share intact. Suspending is never deleting - the partner's id is on every row they wrote.
